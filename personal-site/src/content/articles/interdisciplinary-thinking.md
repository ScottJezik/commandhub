Interdisciplinary thinking is often described as an abstract virtue. In practice, it is a way of noticing what a single field is likely to miss.

Engineering can explain how a system works. Social science can help explain how people and institutions respond to it. History can show how similar arrangements developed. Geopolitics can reveal how power and dependence shape the available choices.

The goal is not to become an expert in everything. It is to know which other lens to reach for when the first one is not enough.
